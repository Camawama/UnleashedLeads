All notable changes to Unleashed Lead are documented in this file.

The format is based on Keep a Changelog (https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/),
and this project adheres to Semantic Versioning (https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html).

## [Unreleased]

### Changed
- Only tamed animals walk along (the owner, 2026-10-13: "Only tamed animals on a lead should walk with the player. Untamed animals should still be difficult to wrangle"). A pet, a tamed horse, donkey, mule, llama or camel, or anything in the new server config list `physics.ledByNature` (allay, camel, Vanilla Backport's happy ghast) keeps its holder's pace (`ledHurry`) and weighs `ledWeight` (0.3) on the lead. An untamed animal walks at vanilla's pace, is dragged on a taut lead and weighs `untamedLedWeight` (1: all of it), so wrangling a cow on a plain lead is real effort again; every tier still divides that by its strength squared (a cow: 650 kg plain, 289 iron, 104 diamond, 41 netherite). An untamed animal towed by a vehicle runs along rather than be dragged (`untamedTowedHurry` 2.0), but still weighs its whole weight on the car on a plain lead.
- A player in creative mode or spectating is never pulled by what they lead (`physics.creativeHoldersHold`, on): it takes the whole pull (the owner: "When I am in creative mode, an animal should not be able to pull me with the lead, especially when in fly mode").
- Gametests `LedPaceTests` (5; 64 in the default suite, with and without `-PvanillaBackport`): the shares (untamed cow 650 kg, on netherite 40.6, tamed horse 135, a creative holder none), an untamed cow not keeping up with a walking player, the slowest tamed horse keeping up walking and sprinting.

### Fixed
- A paused game no longer flings a car or a player on a taut lead (the owner, 2026-10-12: "I paused my game and tabbed out ... when I unpaused I was just flung hundreds of blocks"). The client's tick event keeps firing while paused, but nothing moves: the pull a client gives the car its player drives (and the player holding a lead) was given again on every one of those ticks and piled up in the car's pushed speed (MKW Mobility's `addedVelocity` through Camafy's `Impulses`). Client pulls now stand down while paused and act at most once per tick of what they move (`LeashPhysics.OncePerTick`, keyed to the entity's own tick count), and the tow rope changes how fast its ends part by at most 2 blocks a tick (an upgraded lead stretched 5 blocks past the tow length could throw a car at 40 m/s). Harness `lead_wrangle` (`pauseCar`, `pausePlayer`).
- Animals towed by a lead turn to face the way they are pulled (`faceHolder`): only mobs in the air or without AI turned, so a cow dragged behind a car on the ground slid along sideways or backwards (with Vanilla Backport a mob on a taut lead also stopped walking, which was the only other thing that turned it). A led mob keeping a moving holder's pace walks to where its holder will be when its walk is next renewed: aimed at where the holder was, it reached the end of its path early and turned round to step back onto it (cows towed behind a car spun round now and then).

### Changed
- Led animals walk along instead of being dragged dead (the owner, 2026-10-12: "it's impossible to play Minecraft like you would in vanilla with my mod, since grabbing an animal with a lead and trying to pull is very difficult ... the higher tier leads should make it easier to wrangle ... a tamed horse on a lead should automatically walk with the player"):
  - A mob walking after its holder keeps the holder's pace, up to `physics.ledHurry` (2.5) times its own leash pace, and keeps walking while the lead is taut (Vanilla Backport's leash tick only walked it while the lead was slack). Tamed horses, donkeys, mules, llamas and camels keep up with a sprinting player whatever their speed, and stop eating or rearing when their holder walks off.
  - On a taut lead by mass such a mob puts only `physics.ledWeight` (0.3) of its weight against the lead; an upgraded lead divides that by its strength squared, so it wrangles easier (a cow: 195 kg on a plain lead, 87 iron, 31 diamond, 12 netherite). Vehicles, boats, mobs without AI, sitting pets and anything in the air keep their whole weight.
  - Gametests `LedPaceTests` (4; 63 in the default suite, all passing with and without `-PvanillaBackport`): a cow's and a cow's on a netherite lead share of a player's pull, a cow keeping up with a player walking 15 blocks (4.69 m/s, 3.1 blocks behind, against vanilla's 1.3 m/s), the slowest tamed horse keeping up with a sprinting player (5.70 m/s, 3.0 behind), and a client pull acting once per entity tick.

### Added
- Upgraded leads (the owner, 2026-10-12: "use a smithing table to 'upgrade' lead with like iron or diamond, etc. Stronger lead types would have a harder time breaking"). At the smithing table, with no template: lead + iron ingot = Iron Lead, Iron Lead + diamond = Diamond Lead, Diamond Lead + netherite ingot = Netherite Lead (survives fire and lava). They are leads in every way (tie mobs, boats, cars and the happy ghast, fences, cleats, fence-to-fence ropes; same free length, pull and tow length) and only harder to break: each stretches its strength times as far past its free length before it snaps, so 15.5 / 22.5 / 33 blocks instead of 12 (server config `upgradedLeads`: `ironStrength` 1.5, `diamondStrength` 2.5, `netheriteStrength` 4.0), and a fence-to-fence rope of one on a ship holds that many times `tethers.breakingStrength`. A carried rope snaps that much later too; the longest rope that can be tied stays 12. What a lead is made of stays with it: tied, moved to a fence or another mob, taken back into a hand, along a chain of mobs, saved with the world, drawn in its colour (client config `rope.ironColor`, `diamondColor`, `netheriteColor`), and dropped as itself when it is untied, snaps or its knot goes. With Vanilla Backport its own leash tick snaps the leads it runs at the same distances (its happy ghast's own 16 included). Network protocol 4 (each upgraded lead's tier, and each rope's). Item textures from vanilla's lead by `tools/gen_lead_textures.py`.
- Gametests `LeadTierTests` (8; 59 in the default suite, all passing with and without `-PvanillaBackport`): the real smithing menu makes each tier with no template (and nothing with a template in its slot), each tier holds where the one below snaps and snaps just past its own break distance (dropping itself), a diamond lead tied by a click stays diamond through a fence, the knot and untying, taking a chain keeps each lead's tier, an iron rope is an iron rope (1.5 times the breaking strength, drops an iron lead), the tier is saved with the entity, and Vanilla Backport's snap distance for a boat and its happy ghast on upgraded leads, and a knot holding only a netherite lead stretched 30 blocks that must not clear itself away as empty (knots, shears, players and the client now look for what a holder holds as far as the longest lead reaches, the strongest tier's break distance plus 8, instead of a fixed 20 blocks; 24 on the client). 68 with `-Ppehkui`, 67 with `-PshipTests`. The Pehkui test `tinyPairLeadSnapsAndPullsAtItsScale` measures the lead's own pull with `massLeads` off for that one step: it had failed since leads by mass (a pig without AI held by a player counts as immovable, so the player took the whole pull).
- Leads by mass (server config `physics.massLeads`, on): a lead pulls both ends, each by the other's share of their masses (Camafy's weights, nothing else), momentum kept. A player leading a mob heavier than themselves is slowed and the mob gives slowly (walking/sprinting at 4.3/5.6 blocks a second free: 3.1/2.0 with a pig, 2.2/0.6 with a cow, 0.24 sprinting with an iron golem, which holds you at the lead's length; a chicken barely slows you); a vehicle or a horse a player rides feels what it tows; a lead to a knot on a Valkyrien Skies ship pulls the ship at the knot (through Camafy's `ShipForces`, so it turns the ship too). A player's share is applied on their own client, where they move. Within 1.5 blocks of the snap distance a lead is a tow rope at full stretch (the ends stop parting and the overstretch is taken back, shared by mass), so a car drags a pig rather than snapping its lead. The server sends a driver the mass of the ship their car is tied to (network protocol 3). Fence knots in the world never move; chains of mobs keep their rules.
- The lead cleat (`unleashedlead:lead_cleat`): a small iron cleat on any face of a block that leads and fence-to-fence ropes tie to as to a fence, with no collision (it snags nothing on a ship's hull) and 1 kg on a Valkyrien Skies ship. Four iron nuggets and a string make two. Its knot sits on its horn and goes with it. Block tag `unleashedlead:lead_anchors` (every fence and the cleat).
- Gametests `LeadCleatTests` (5; 51 in the default suite): tying a held pig by using a cleat, the knot on the horn lasting through vanilla's survival checks, the knot going with its cleat, a lead used on a cleat while sneaking, no collision on any face, a rope from cleat to cleat pulling at the horns.
- Chains of mobs pull both ways (server config `features.mobChains`, on). A lead between two mobs now pulls whichever end is further from what moves the chain, so you can drag a chain by any of its mobs and the rest follow, through any number of links:
  - what moves a chain: a mob held by a player, a fence knot or a boat, and a mob the lead can't move at all (one another mod moves, such as Physgun Unbound's grab or an MKW kart; a sitting pet; a ride a player steers; a mob with no AI; a mob riding something);
  - for each lead between two mobs, the end nearer (counted in leads along the chain) stays put for that lead and the far end gets the pull a mob gets from a player today: the same spring, damper and cap, measured against the near end's own motion. The far end also walks after the near one, and the near one no longer walks back toward the far one;
  - two mobs equally far from anything that moves them (a free chain wandering about, or a tug of war) pull each other, shared by Camafy's masses: a chicken tied to a pig moves fifty times as much as the pig;
  - a lead held by a player, a knot or a boat is still never pulled at its holder's end, and a chain held by a player at its top behaves exactly as before.
- A lead used on a mob that another mob holds takes the whole chain instead of untying it (`mobChains`): the mob goes on your lead and every lead from it up the chain is turned to hang from the mob below, so it now leads the chain and nothing drops. A mob can have only one holder, so this is how a chain is held by any of its mobs. On a chain you already hold by its other end, your lead just moves to this mob (no new lead used). A chain tied to a fence or held by another player is taken over as before (just this mob; its old lead drops).
- Gametests `ChainLeashTests` (9, in their own batch `chains`; 46 in the default suite): a player walking off with the top of a two-pig chain; the bottom pig grabbed (a MOTION claim, as Physgun Unbound grabs) and dragged at walking pace and at a sprint; three-pig chains grabbed at the bottom, middle and top and sprinted 13 blocks; a lead on the bottom pig of a free chain, of a chain you hold and of a chain on a fence; a chain taken by its bottom pig then walked off; one tick of a grabbed pig's lead (the holder gets the whole pull, the grabbed pig none); a chicken and a pig sharing the pull by mass; players and knots never pulled. With `mobChains` off, seven of them fail exactly as reported: the lead snaps at 12.4 blocks a second or two after the dragging starts.
- Fence-to-fence ropes. Use a lead on a fence, then use another fence (or its knot) within 12 blocks: a rope is tied between them, as long as the distance plus half a block of slack.
  - Using the first fence again takes the lead back. Using a knot with an empty hand takes its ropes' ends into your hand. Shears, a dispenser with shears, a broken or pushed fence, or a punched knot cut them, one lead dropped per rope. A carried end snaps past 12 blocks and breaks with a firework boost.
  - Between two fences in the world the rope just hangs. Knots with a rope on them are never cleared away as empty.
  - Saved with the world; an end whose fence isn't loaded waits (the rope goes slack) and nothing is loaded to find it. Synced to clients and drawn with the simulated rope.
- Mooring Valkyrien Skies ships. A rope from a ship's fence to a fence in the world, or to another ship, holds the ship back:
  - slack up to its length, then a spring and damper along the rope, applied at the knot (a ship tied at its bow swings around it);
  - stiffer for heavier ships, but less than in proportion, so a small boat is held firmly and a big ship stretches the rope;
  - between two ships, equal and opposite;
  - several ropes add up, and each snaps on its own when its pull passes the breaking strength (the lead drops where it broke, with the "Lead snaps" sound);
  - computed on Valkyrien Skies' physics thread through Camafy's ship force hook.
- Server config `tethers`: `enabled`, `stiffness` (20000 N per block on a 1 t ship, times the square root of the mass in tonnes), `damping` (0.5 of critical), `breakingStrength` (1,000,000 N), `slack` (0.5 blocks).
- Leads on mobs resized by Pehkui (bundled in Genesis) or another size mod: a shrunk or grown mob is led like any other (see Fixed). The free length and snap distance are measured from an enlarged body rather than its centre, so a big mob held at its side has its whole lead; at normal size or smaller nothing changes.
- `-Ppehkui` puts Pehkui 3.8.2 on the dev runtime and runs nine more gametests (46 in all): a 1/16 pig and a 4x pig led by a walking player, a 1/16 pig led by a 1/16 player, the pull per tick at three sizes, snapping past 12, the lead's size for each pair of ends, a 1/16 pair's lead snapping past 12/16 and pulling past 5/16, collar and hand points, and Camafy's weights at every size.
- Gametests: tying through the real block use, the off-hand second use, taking back and untying, the carried snap, shears and broken fences, reloading, and the lead's size rule (a rope at 1/16 is exactly the normal rope at 1/16; normal ends, babies and knots give exactly the normal lead) (37 in the default suite). With `-PshipTests`, Valkyrien Skies goes on the dev runtime and five tests moor real ships: one rope stops a pushed raft, three ropes stretch less than one, a hard push snaps a rope, a ship-to-ship rope pulls both, and a ship rope survives a reload. `-PvsBackend=classic|physx|jolt` picks VS's physics backend.

### Changed
- Using a fence with a lead in your hand and nothing to tie now starts a rope, even if the fence's knot has leads on it. To take those leads, use the knot with an empty hand, as before.
- Knot use is handled in the entity interaction event instead of by cancelling `LeashFenceKnotEntity.interact` at its head.
- `LeadItem.bindPlayerMobs` is wrapped whole instead of cancelled at its head, and the fence and lead uses pass on which hand was used.
- The pathfinding-mob leash tick no longer returns from the middle of `PathfinderMob.tickLeash`; it takes over by handing vanilla's code no holder.
- The knot tick check no longer cancels `HangingEntity.tick`, which Pehkui also injects into.
- The network protocol version is now 4 (upgraded leads' tiers; 3 was the knot masses, 2 a new message).
- Zero and low gravity (VS Genesis's space, Ad Astra's orbits and planets, Gravity Unbound's zero-strength fields): ropes sag in proportion to the local gravity (Camafy 0.1.7's `Gravity.ambientStrength`), so in space slack floats in a sideways bow instead of hanging, and on the Moon a rope settles six times slower into the same curve. A weightless mob on a lead is pulled along the rope in every direction, and a Pehkui-resized weightless mob in real blocks on every axis (there is no weight for the lead to lift). Needs Camafy 0.1.7.
- Needs Camafy 0.1.6 or later (below 0.2), for `compat.scale.EntityScale`.

### Fixed
- **A chain of ships on ropes never settled** (the owner: "If I connect a bunch of ships together in a row with lead, and hold one of the ships up and freeze it in air, they all start moving sporadically and the movement of the lead never settle"). A fence-to-fence (cleat-to-cleat) rope pulled a ship only along the rope, a spring and a damper on the stretch, and only while taut; nothing took out the sideways swing of one end against the other, and Valkyrien Skies' air drag on a ship is far too weak to, so a hanging chain swung and snatched for good. Ropes now also damp that sideways motion while taut (`tether/TetherPhysics`, server config `tethers.swayDamping`, 4.0 per second; 0 = the old behaviour): the other knot's velocity across the rope, times the rate and the mass that motion meets at the two knots, equal and opposite on the two ships (each ship's own physics hook adds its half, as for the pull), capped like the rope's damper so it stays stable at VS's step however light the ship. Measured (pack harness `grav_lead_chain`: six plank ships of 500 to 4,500 kg on ropes, the first frozen 40 blocks up, the rest let go to hang; mean kinetic energy per 5 s window over 30 s, and the fastest ship in the last window): without it 322,521 / 61,488 / 35,926 / 25,813 / 20,549 / 22,091 J (the swing never dies down; a ship still at 7.5 blocks/s after 30 s); at 1.5, 3,570 J and 2.5 blocks/s in the last window; at 4.0 (the default) 297,102 / 36,895 / 10,801 / 5,290 / 1,992 / 1,326 J, 1.4 blocks/s, still falling. The pull along the rope and the breaking strength are unchanged (Unleashed Lead's 59 gametests with `-PshipTests` pass).
- Two mobs tied together could only be dragged by the holder (the owner's report of 2026-10-01). A lead only ever pulled the mob it was tied to toward whatever held it, so dragging the tied mob away (with Physgun Unbound, or by anything else) left the holder standing and stretched the lead until it snapped at 12; and using a lead on the tied mob took it over and dropped its lead to the other mob. See Added (`mobChains`).
- The Pehkui suite's `scaledPigsStillSnapPast12` failed whenever its plot fell on a chunk that wasn't loaded yet (its far pig, spawned there, was never added to the world). It now places its pigs once the whole plot ticks.
- A lead between a player and a pig both shrunk to 1/16 was a normal-sized lead: as thick as the pig, five blocks long with its slack in a loop around them, and snapping 12 blocks away. Only its ends followed the size. A lead is now sized for the two things it joins (Pehkui or any other size mod; vanilla babies and fence knots count as normal size):
  - its thickness (width and collision radius) follows the smaller end, so a tiny player's lead on a normal pig, or a normal player's on a tiny pig, is a thin cord;
  - its lengths follow the larger end, never above normal: the free length, the snap distance, the rope's slack, segment spacing, payout speed and fall speed (so it sags and swings like a normal rope seen smaller), how close a mob walks up to its holder, and when a horse or camel reacts. Between two ends at 1/16 the lead is 5/16 of a block free and snaps past 12/16. With a normal-sized end or a fence, the lengths stay normal.
  - Vanilla Backport is handed the distance on a normal-sized lead, so its own thresholds scale the same way.
  - Leads between normal-sized ends are unchanged.
- A mob shrunk with Pehkui lost its lead as soon as you walked off with it. Pehkui moves a mob by its motion scale times its velocity, so a pig at 1/16 moved 1/16 of what the lead gave it (at most 0.04 blocks a tick against a walking player's 0.2) and fell behind until the lead snapped at 12. The pull now makes up for the motion scale across the ground, so it keeps up. Up and down it doesn't, so a tiny pig stays on the ground instead of being flown like a kite. A pig grown to 4 was pulled four times too hard, and now isn't.
- The rope left a resized player at vanilla's fixed hand point, a block below the top of the player's box: under the feet of a player shrunk to 1/16, at the shoulders of one grown to 2. It now leaves the hand at any size. Collars already followed the size.
- With Vanilla Backport, the first leash distance read in a session could switch off the whole bridge to its leash model (boats, happy ghast) for that session: the bridge was linked after its method handle had been read.
- Vanilla's leash line is skipped at its call instead of cancelling `MobRenderer.renderLeash` at its head: with Pehkui (bundled in Genesis), the first leashed mob drawn crashed the game with "Pose stack not empty". MixinExtras is now bundled in the release jar.
- With an empty main hand, tying held leads to a fence could take them straight back: the client's follow-up off-hand use in the same tick counted as a second use.
- Knots on Valkyrien Skies ships are found again when used, instead of a second knot being made on the same fence (Valkyrien Skies turns entity searches in the shipyard into searches around the ship in the world). Boats restoring a saved lead to a ship's fence use the same lookup.
- A mob tied to a fence on a Valkyrien Skies ship lost its lead (dropped next to it) when you went away, to another dimension or far enough off, and came back. Valkyrien Skies loads and unloads a ship's chunks by your distance to the ship, apart from the world chunks the mob stands in, so the mob went on ticking after its knot had unloaded with the ship, and vanilla drops a lead whose holder is gone. Coming back, the mob could also load first: vanilla made a knot in the ship's unloaded shipyard at once, and a lead measured to a ship that isn't loaded read millions of blocks and snapped. Now:
  - a lead whose knot unloads with its ship is kept as a saved lead, and tied back to the fence's knot once the ship and the fence's chunk are loaded again, however long that takes;
  - until then it neither pulls nor snaps, and no knot is made on the fence;
  - it still drops if the fence is gone when the ship is back, or the ship no longer exists.
  - Boats wait the same way. With Vanilla Backport (its own boat leads), a boat's lead no longer snaps while its ship loads, but still drops if its knot unloads first. Leads to fences in the world are unchanged.
  - Gametests: a lead saved to a fence in the world is still tied back at once; with `-PshipTests`, a pig tied to a raft keeps its lead while the raft's chunks unload, and it and a pig loaded in the meantime are tied back to one knot when they load.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-09-27

First release: Bedrock-style leads and the Java 1.21 to 1.21.6 lead changes on Forge 1.20.1.

### Added
- Rope rendering. Every lead is a simulated rope (position-based dynamics) that:
  - sags along local gravity, sways, and pays out and reels in slack;
  - drapes over blocks (fences at their visible height) and wraps around posts;
  - is twice vanilla's thickness, with a square cross-section, the two-tone stripes and light taken at each point along it;
  - is interpolated between ticks and pinned exactly to hand and collar every frame;
  - is culled by its own bounds, not by either end;
  - is simulated only near the camera (distance and count limits). Further ropes are drawn as a fixed curve.
- Elastic leash physics:
  - free within 5 blocks, a spring and outward damper past it, snapping at 12, measured centre to centre;
  - a mob hanging from a knot swings like a pendulum;
  - no fall damage builds up while a lead carries an entity;
  - dragged entities turn toward the holder;
  - a player-steered boat is not pulled (optional).
- Leashable boats, chest boats and bamboo rafts: saved with the world, synced to clients, and tied to fences like mobs.
- Mob-to-mob leads (sneak-use a mob while holding leashed mobs).
- Shears cut an entity's lead and every lead tied to it. A dispenser with shears does it too.
- Using a lead on an entity tied to a knot or a mob takes it over. Leads can't be taken from another player.
- 1.21.6 knot rules: using a knot or fence with nothing to tie takes its leads, sneak-use only ties, and empty knots disappear.
- Knots break the moment their fence is removed.
- A firework boost while gliding breaks the leads you hold.
- The 1.21.6 lead recipe (5 string, 2 leads), toggleable, with the old recipe as fallback.
- Sounds with subtitles: "Lead tied", "Lead untied", "Lead snaps" and "Shears snip", using vanilla's own leash-knot audio.
- Common, server and client config files for every feature and every tuning value.
- Built on Camafy:
  - gravity bridge (Gravity Unbound);
  - Valkyrien Skies bridge: knots on ship fences work, and ropes are drawn in world space;
  - physics ownership: entities whose motion another mod owns are never leashed or pulled, and Full Stop gets a G_FORCE claim while a lead is taut;
  - minigame policies;
  - network channel and config helpers.
- Vanilla Backport compatibility:
  - Vanilla Backport keeps its leash model, happy ghast and quad-lead physics.
  - Unleashed Lead plugs its pull, distances and ship handling into Vanilla Backport's hooks, draws every lead (Vanilla Backport's renderer is switched off), handles lead interactions first, and wins the recipe.
- Gametests, 23 in total: snap distance, free length, pendulum, fall damage, motion claims, boats (tie, save/load, drag and turn, fence), shears, dispensers, mob-to-mob, take-over, knots, fireworks, the recipe shape, the rope solver (length, drape, moving end) and the Vanilla Backport split. Run plain and with `-PvanillaBackport`.
